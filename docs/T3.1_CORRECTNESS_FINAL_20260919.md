## Three real attempts to close it, all refuted

**1. Hypothesis: the "second region" (appended, separately-assembled
leftover reads) is where the bloat lives — exclude it and correctness
improves.**

Tested by splitting the export into `pg_main` (10.77 Mb, the genuinely
overlap-chained pseudogenome) and `pg_second` (41.72 Mb, the appended pool)
and re-scoring `pg_main` alone against the same reference.

**Result: refuted.** Genome fraction identical (80.808%, to three decimal
places), mismatch rate identical (15.03/100kbp), duplication ratio
identical (1.432). The second region contributes almost nothing to the
aligned portion (it is 90%+ unaligned on its own), so removing it only
shrinks the *unaligned byte count*, not the correctness metrics. **The
correctness gap is intrinsic to the main pseudogenome construction itself,
not something that can be split away.**

**2. Hypothesis: a stricter chaining overlap threshold (MINOV) produces a
tighter, less redundant, more genome-like main pseudogenome.**

Tested directly: `best106 reads.fq 3 90 22 16 16 1 24 64 1` (MINOV=90,
vs. the archive-size-optimal MINOV=52 chosen automatically by
`encode_adaptive.sh`'s own search).

**Result: refuted, badly.** `PG_LEN` grew to 138,905,031 bp — **30x the true
genome**, worse than the original 52.5 Mb by a factor of 2.6. Requiring
longer overlaps means fewer reads chain together, so more reads spill into
separate, redundant segments. Stricter is not tighter here; it is more
fragmented.

**3. Hypothesis: the opposite — a much more permissive overlap threshold
merges more reads together and shrinks the pseudogenome toward the true
genome size.**

Tested: `best106 reads.fq 3 12 22 16 16 1 24 64 1` (MINOV=12).

**Result: also refuted.** `PG_LEN` = 132,657,233 bp — still ~28x the true
genome, essentially as bad as the stricter direction.

**Correction, found and fixed in this session: attempts 2 and 3 above were
run with a methodological bug and their conclusions were wrong.** The
direct `best106` invocations dropped one argument from the real 10-token
template `encode_adaptive.sh` uses, silently shifting every parameter after
MINOV by one position and leaving `MAXMAP` at its uncalibrated default
(11, from `Lmax/13`) instead of the archive-optimal value (30) the adaptive
search actually selects. That confound, not MINOV, explains why both
directions looked catastrophic (138.9 Mb and 132.7 Mb). Re-run correctly —
full 10-argument template, `MAXMAP` explicitly held at 30 via the
documented `MAXMAP` env override — MINOV swept from 20 to 120 changes
`PG_LEN` by less than 3% (52.5-54.1 Mb throughout). **MINOV alone is not
the lever. This correction matters and is kept here rather than silently
replaced, per this project's own rule that retractions stay in the docs.**

## The real lever, found by testing MAXMAP correctly

`MAXMAP` (the pigeonhole-mapping mismatch ceiling for placing leftover
reads onto the already-built pseudogenome) turned out to be the actual
control, and it is not a wash — swept properly (`MINOV=52` held fixed):

| MAXMAP | PG_LEN | archive |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 92,685,638 | 10,799,150 |
| 11 (default) | 76,311,388 | 8,745,841 |
| 30 (archive-size-optimal, the original test) | 52,489,692 | 5,796,005 |
| 60 | 16,344,845 | 2,557,445 |
| 90 | 15,508,295 | 2,403,580 |
| 100 | 15,383,495 | 2,374,227 |

Higher `MAXMAP` shrinks **both** the export and the archive simultaneously
— a genuine win-win on this axis, not a trade-off. The mechanism: more
leftover reads get mapped onto existing pseudogenome content instead of
being appended as new, redundant sequence in the second region. The curve
plateaus around `MAXMAP=90-100`.

**Separately, `MINOV` DOES matter for the main region specifically** —
isolated by holding `MAXMAP=90` and reading `pg after chains` (the main
region alone, before the second region is added): it shrinks steadily from
11,872,011 bp (MINOV=20) to 10,232,526 bp (MINOV=90), then plateaus and
slightly reverses at MINOV=108 (the read-length ceiling). A real, modest,
~14% effect — not the >20x swings the broken test wrongly suggested.

## The one remaining candidate idea — already tried, already rejected

Before concluding, one more real option was considered: a self-alignment
pass over the exported pseudogenome to detect and merge near-duplicate
segments, re-mapping affected read placements to the collapsed
coordinates. This was **not attempted** here, because it is not new —
`CLAUDE.md` already documents it, tried twice in this project's history
(second-region self-match against itself, not just against the main pg),
quantified as a real win in principle (E. coli: archive -61,792 to
-95,373 B) and **reverted both times because it made the archive LOSSY**
(commit `d1812f5`). Read reconstruction does not currently honour
references whose source sits in a collapsed/merged region. Attempting it
again now, under time pressure, to close this specific gap would mean
repeating a documented, known-bad change rather than doing new, safe
engineering. That is not a responsible trade against the one guarantee
(57/57 losslessness across 19 datasets) the entire project's Claim 1
stands on.

## A structural attempt, not just parameters: post-export self-alignment

All of the above is parameter tuning within the existing pipeline. A
genuinely different, structural idea was tested too: self-align the
exported FASTA against itself (`minimap2 -x asm5 -D`) to find near-duplicate
contigs or segments that could be collapsed into one, as a safe
post-processing step that never touches the archive's own decode path (so
it carries no risk to the 57/57 lossless guarantee, unlike the self-match
idea above).

**Result: no clean signal to collapse.** 5,873 non-trivial self-alignment
hits were found, but every one of them is weak — matched bases are only
2-5% of the aligned span, not the >90% identity a genuine collapsible
duplicate would show. If the redundancy were "the same region assembled
twice, nearly identically," self-alignment would find long, high-identity
blocks. It does not.

**What this reveals about the actual structure of the problem:** the
redundancy is not duplicate *sequence* sitting in two places — it is
chaining fragmenting one true genomic locus into *multiple divergent paths*
(different reads, different local sequencing errors, chained into slightly
different routes through what should be one region). Those paths do not
align cleanly to *each other*; they would only align cleanly to the *true
reference*, which a reference-free system does not have. There is no
simple post-processing fix for this, because there is no clean duplicate
signal to post-process. Closing this requires the same thing the parameter
search already pointed to — graph-aware repeat resolution that tracks
multiple divergent paths as one locus and merges by coverage/consensus —
not a dedup pass.

**Four independent approaches, two different levels of the problem
(parameter tuning and structural post-processing), all converge on the
same conclusion.** This is not a parameter-search dead end alone — it is
an architectural boundary, confirmed from a genuinely different direction.

## What this means, honestly

**Dominant correctness is not achievable by tuning parameters of the

**This is not a failure to find the answer — it is the answer.** Per this

# CORRECTED RESULT — the export was the bug

## What was wrong with every number above

`export` writes the pseudogenome as **two** FASTA records (`capsule_pg_main`,
`capsule_pg_second`). The pseudogenome is not two contigs. On E. coli at
MAXMAP=90/MINOV=90 it is **41,603 contigs**, and the default export
concatenates them end to end. QUAST therefore saw one 10.7 Mb "contig"
whose sequence jumps discontinuously roughly every 360 bases. That is what
produced the whole body of this document: 80.8% genome fraction, 90%
unaligned, `Largest alignment` of only 17,984 bp against a 10.7 Mb
"contig", and `LA50 = 413`. Those were artifacts of the export format.

The fix required no new code and no algorithm change:

    CAPS_CALL=1 ... best106 ...            # writes contig_spans
    CAPSULE_EXPORT_CONTIGS=1 x_decode export ...   # emits real contigs

**Archive re-verified LOSSLESS after the change** (3,106,518 reads in,
3,106,518 out, byte-identical, via `mp_decode` with the standard
`DUMP_PERM/DUMP_MM/DUMP_LIT/CAPS_SPANS` flags). The export path does not
touch read reconstruction, so this was expected, but it was checked rather
than assumed.

# Two further attempts after the export fix — both refuted, both informative

## 1. Redundancy removal on the real contigs

Now possible, because there are real contigs to compare (it was meaningless

| metric | per-contig | + dedup | SPAdes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genome fraction | **98.470%** | 98.213% | 98.346% |
| Duplication ratio | 1.990 | **1.320** | 1.000 |
| Unaligned length | 769,093 | **309,470** | 9,582 |
| Mismatches /100 kbp | 11.86 | 11.06 | 2.75 |
| N50 | 1,996 | 2,687 | 139,621 |

It relocates the gap rather than closing it: duplication and unaligned
improve, genome fraction drops just below SPAdes. **Per-contig without dedup
remains the best configuration.**

## 2. Consensus polishing from the archive's own pileup — REFUTED, and the
## reason is the interesting part

Hypothesis: SPAdes runs BayesHammer error correction before assembling; our
pseudogenome is built from raw reads and keeps their errors, which is what
inflates our mismatch rate. The archive already stores a per-position
deviation tally (`.sites`). Majority-vote polishing the EXPORT from it
should outvote individual read errors, export-only, no risk to the archive.

Measured, E. coli, `>=10`-read support threshold:

| | unpolished | polish, `.sites` as-is | polish, `.sites` strand-corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| positions changed | - | 21,405 | 55,901 |
| Mismatches /100 kbp | **11.86** | 72.63 | **407.06** |
| Indels /100 kbp | **0.23** | 0.63 | 6.78 |
| Genome fraction | **98.470%** | 98.434% | 97.959% |

**The better the offsets, the worse the result.** That is the finding. A
strand-correction fix for the `.sites` tally was written, built and gated
(archive LOSSLESS, export byte-identical), and it correctly recovers more
genuine deviation sites -- and polishing them does proportionally MORE
damage. So the failure is not bad coordinates.

**Root cause: this is not a biological pileup.** Read placement in a
pseudogenome is chosen to minimise encoded bits, not to reflect homology.
At a repeat-collapsed position, reads originating from a DIFFERENT genomic
copy are placed in and vote with their own bases, so the majority is not the
true base for that locus. Consensus polishing is a valid operation on an
assembler's pileup and an invalid one here. This is a structural property of
compression-driven placement, not a defect to be fixed.

## The strand inconsistency in `.sites`, recorded and deliberately left open

`.sites` tallies deviations at the raw offset `j`, while `query` and the
`.xmi` verifier both strand-correct to `q+RL-1-j` for reverse-complement
reads. That is a real inconsistency and the one-line fix is known, builds,
and passes both gates (archive LOSSLESS, export byte-identical).

**It was reverted rather than adopted**, because `.sites` feeds the
native-pileup fast path whose published numbers are in `CLAUDE.md` (400/400
het at >=2 reads, 25 homozygous false positives, 19.6x faster than the read
path). Changing the tally can move those numbers, so adopting the fix
requires re-running and re-gating that claim -- a scoped piece of work, not
an end-of-session edit. The fix and this reasoning are recorded in a comment
at the tally site in `stages/capsule_decode.cpp`.
